Methodology. "Training throughput" and "GPU setup" are quoted directly from the source when the project reports them. "Est. training GPU-hours" is only quoted directly when the source states it explicitly; otherwise it's computed as GPU count × wall-clock training time and marked "computed" so it's clear it's a derived estimate, not a reported figure.

TinyLlama. GPU-hours are computed (16 × 90 days × 24h = ~34,560). The paper independently reports 3,456 GPU-hours for a 300B-token subset on the same hardware, which scales linearly to ~34,560 GPU-hours for the full 3T-token run — consistent with the computed figure above.

ProLong. Unlike TinyLlama, ProLong's paper (Table 9) states GPU-hours directly per stage, so no computation was needed there. Throughput figures here are back-computed from those reported GPU-hours (tokens ÷ GPU-hours); the paper separately reports per-device throughput for its 64K-stage ablation runs (Table 15: 2,770–3,095 tok/s/GPU depending on optimizations), which brackets the computed 64K figure above. The 512K stage is far slower per-GPU because distributed (sequence-parallel) attention is only engaged at that length. SFT (1B tokens on UltraChat) is included in the token count above but not in the 14,400 GPU-hours figure, since the paper doesn't state GPU-hours for the SFT stage separately.

Sources: TinyLlama GitHub repo and the TinyLlama paper (arXiv:2401.02385); ProLong GitHub repo and the ProLong paper (arXiv:2410.02660).
